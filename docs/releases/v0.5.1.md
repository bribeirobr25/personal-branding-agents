# Method 0.5.1 release candidate

These Discovery rules come from a test of method 0.5.0. Method 0.5.1 keeps the two-part Discovery gate, `validated_for_strategy`, and the rule that a correction and a closed gate do not share a reply. De-duplication of repeated rules is deferred to 0.6.

- **Closing Discovery.** When the gate closes, present the confirmed profile, each held-out item by name and what would resolve it, open questions still waiting plus the positioning questions that come next (named, not asked), two or three directions labelled as possibilities to explore, not a positioning, and one question, then wait. Never write "no blockers" while one is open. No unrequested positioning statements: labelled directions with support and open questions are allowed at this step; promises, taglines, bios, pillar lists and plans are not. Silence, "ok", or praise is not a choice. If the person asks what else you need, give the summary to confirm, the held-out items and the end-step question. Positioning questions wait.
- **Reading public presence,** in order: the assistant's own tools; other tools by type, each with a named list, the person's yes, and read-only use; the person's own export or paste, offered early for LinkedIn; then an honest account of what is still missing, including a file that will not open. Read-only use during Discovery follows that guide. Publishing connections still wait for an approved channel strategy.
- When material involves someone else's work, business or details, ask once whether that person agrees to its use. A mention, praise, or listing it as something they share is not that person's yes. Until answered, list it as held out.
- Keep the Discovery checkpoint in the conversation. Say it was saved only if a tool actually saved it; otherwise offer a copy the person can keep. Do not claim memory or background work that does not exist.
- During Discovery, an observation about the person, linked to evidence, is fine. A promise, pillars, an audience choice, a bio or a plan waits until positioning, after the person chooses a direction at Closing Discovery.
- If the person's goal depends on others seeing their work, ask once before the gate for two or three things they are comfortable showing publicly. That ask is not a prerequisite for the gate.
- The future-self examples include "You can skip this or come back to it later." The question is not a prerequisite. If they name several things they do not want to be, ask about all of them in one short question.
- If a named link opens only through a signed-in or connected tool, or might be private, stop and ask with a named list before reading it.

Method 0.5.1 retains contract version 0.2.0 and the existing templates. It does not change the shared lifecycle or approval records. De-duplication is deferred to 0.6.

The public checkout contains the reusable framework, the practical guide, and fictional examples. Its CI checks documentation, contracts, and templates.

## Adopt or roll back

Method 0.5.1 retains the 0.2.0 record contracts and templates. The Discovery checkpoint remains local to onboarding and is kept in the conversation. Continue using the existing shared lifecycle and approval records. For the 0.5.0 onboarding behavior, see [method 0.5.0](v0.5.0.md). For older records, see [migration to 0.2.0](v0.2.0.md).

After publication, pin the release tag and full commit SHA in your private workspace. Review the updated Discovery prompts against local overrides before adopting them. Roll back by restoring the prior pin and compatible overrides.
