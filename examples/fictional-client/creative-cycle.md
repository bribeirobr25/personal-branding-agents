# Fictional creative-cycle walkthrough

Illustrative artifact, not an executed evaluation or measured result. This example was independently invented for the public method. It is not an anonymized client.

## Context and proof

Sol is a fictional beginner translating a completely invented tabletop game's rules. A synthetic glossary draft contained six terms; the next draft contains eight and explains two previously ambiguous labels. No player study, paid client, faster translation or sales result exists. Desired outcome: learn to communicate careful terminology decisions to peers. Constraints: text only, low time budget and no claim of professional expertise.

GoalMap: reputation for transparent learning, earned through showing a specific decision and its limits. Interest Bank records the experiment. EvidenceRecord supports only the synthetic before/after glossary, not its effect on players. The current voice is straightforward and avoids superlatives.

## Three concepts

| ID | Different premise | Opening candidate | Proof/payoff | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Demonstrate an ambiguity | “Two labels looked interchangeable. I wrote down where they differ.” | Show the two invented definitions and the decision | No claim that players understood them better |
| B | Explain evidence limits | “The glossary is clearer to me. That is not a player test.” | Describe what changed and a possible next test | Personal judgment is not measured impact |
| C | Teach a small procedure | “Before adding another definition, identify the decision a player needs to make.” | Explain a three-question review procedure using the invented terms | Advice is a proposed method, not proven performance |

Illustrative selection: A for a peer demonstration. B remains a useful follow-up about testing; C requires enough context to avoid sounding universal. Scores would need recorded reasons under the rubric; this table does not claim a scored run or audience preference.

## Draft and gates

Draft excerpt: “I am practicing on a fictional game's glossary. Two labels seemed similar, so I added definitions explaining their different uses. This shows my editorial decision, not evidence that players now understand the rules better. My next step would be to test the wording with readers.”

Factual scope: synthetic exercise and personal interpretation only. Strategic value: a concrete learning process for peers. Clarity: one example with an explicit limit. Rights/privacy: invented game and person, no external assets. Authenticity/channel: beginner voice in text. These are illustrative review considerations, not a real independent five-PASS report.

No owner approval, scheduled action, publication, performance metric or revenue exists. Approval stays null; state stays drafted. A fictional hypothesis is that peers can identify the distinction after reading; it has not been tested.

## Rejection example

“Eight definitions doubled our sales” is BLOCK because the fixture contains no sales data. Three rewrites of that claim would not create three valid concepts. The next action is to remove the claim, not assign a high creativity score.
