Understood—five minutes, no intake questions. I’ll treat “problem-solving contracts” as paid engagements where clients hire you to diagnose and resolve a specific business problem.

**Lead with a costly problem, not “I’m a problem solver.”** That description is too broad for a buyer to recognize when to hire you.

Use this provisional offer:

> I help **[type of client]** resolve **[specific recurring problem]**. We start with a fixed-scope diagnostic: identify the bottleneck, assess the options, and deliver a practical action plan. Implementation is a separate engagement if needed.

### A simple contract ladder
1. **Paid diagnostic:** defined inputs, a findings brief, prioritized recommendations, and a readout.
2. **Implementation sprint:** one agreed problem, bounded scope, clear deliverables and acceptance criteria.
3. **Ongoing support:** only if there is a genuine recurring need.

Promise deliverables you control—not guaranteed revenue, savings, or transformation.

### A message you can adapt today
> I’m taking on focused engagements to help [client type] tackle [problem]. The starting point is a paid diagnostic with clear findings and next steps—not an open-ended consulting commitment. If this is a current priority, I can send a short scope.
