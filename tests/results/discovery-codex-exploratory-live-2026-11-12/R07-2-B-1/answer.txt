For problem-solving contracts, make it easy for a buyer to recognize **a specific problem you can solve**, rather than presenting yourself as a general “problem solver.”

Here’s a five-minute exercise—no questions or research needed:

**1. Choose one problem you’ve actually handled.**  
Prefer something with a clear cost: delayed delivery, recurring errors, wasted effort, or stalled decisions.

**2. Write a bounded offer.**  
> “I help [type of team] address [specific problem]. My initial engagement delivers [diagnosis, prioritized plan, or tested fix] within [realistic timeframe].”

Promise a deliverable you control, not an outcome you can’t guarantee.

**3. Add one truthful proof point.**  
> “On [relevant project], my role was [your contribution], and the observed result was [supportable result].”

No reliable metric? Describe what changed without inventing a number.

**4. Use it in one relevant conversation.**  
> “I’m taking on focused contracts around [problem]. If that’s a current issue for your team, I can outline a small initial engagement to assess it and recommend next steps.”

This is a provisional starting point—not a validated positioning statement, since I don’t yet know your experience. You don’t need to rebuild your public profile before testing whether the offer resonates.
